Sales, finance and stock ledger for an apparel brand, inside their CRM

An apparel brand sells through a WooCommerce store in several currencies and through distributors. Orders reached the CRM, but the owner had no trustworthy revenue in local currency, no sales by channel or country, and distributor stock was tracked by hand. The CRM's own payment records disagreed with the store on statuses and refunds.

  • RoleSolo: data model, Worker, dashboards, PDF documents, audits with the owner
  • ScaleAbout 720 orders synced, full history backfilled, 8 waybill templates
  • Quality136 tests; every figure audited against the store's order cards
  • StackWorkers, D1, cron triggers, WooCommerce REST, HighLevel custom objects, ECharts

What I built

  • One ledger, one source of truth. A Worker with a D1 database syncs orders every 15 minutes from the store and the CRM. The store decides status, payment, refunds and country; the CRM only mirrors it. Mismatches are listed, never silently merged.
  • Money that adds up. Each order is converted to local currency at the central-bank rate on the payment date. Acquiring fees and shipping are separate fields, so revenue equals the sum of line items and the accountant can reconcile it.
  • CRM custom objects (Sales, Finance ledger, Products, Stock movements) linked to contacts and companies, plus customer metrics written to the contact: total spent, average order, frequency, VIP / sleeping status.
  • Dashboards: native CRM dashboards on the objects, and an embedded page with tabs (Store / Distributors / Finance / Customers) and cascading filters by product, country, currency and acquisition channel from the store's attribution.
  • Distributor module: shipments, returns, stock counts and payments; printable PDF waybills generated on the Worker without a PDF library (8 templates); a "New company" form that validates legal details.

Result

  • In production since September 2026 on the agency's Cloudflare account; full order history backfilled.
  • Since mid-October the stock in the CRM mirrors the website; writing stock back to the store is built and waits behind a feature flag.
  • The owner reads one dashboard whose numbers match the store.
