AI phone support line wired into a helpdesk

A US fleet-management SaaS gets support calls around the clock from drivers and fleet owners, many of them not in English. Two voice agents answer the phones: a support line and a safety line. My job was to make them reliable and cheap, and to turn calls into work the team can act on.

  • RoleOwner of the voice stack: prompts, n8n flows, monitoring, cost and QA reporting
  • Volume2,478 calls in 1–21 Aug 2026; ~4,280 agent minutes a month
  • LanguagesFour on the safety line, chosen in the first seconds of the call
  • StackElevenLabs agents, n8n, Zammad, Telegram, Cloudflare Workers, Gemini / GPT

What the line does

  • The agent answers, picks the caller's language first (a prompt rewrite that fixed mixed-language replies on live calls), looks up the customer and handles the common requests.
  • Each call goes through n8n: classified, deduplicated and routed. Real requests become helpdesk tickets; urgent ones also go to Telegram.
  • Silent calls from known customers are tagged for a callback; empty calls are closed automatically, so nobody triages noise.
  • A daily QA digest (a Worker) summarizes calls by topic and flags the ones a human should listen to.

Operations work

  • n8n audit: reviewed 139 workflows, found a zombie firing about 1,440 false runs a day and removed it, added one error workflow that alerts several Telegram recipients and keeps going if one of them is blocked. Delivery tested end to end.
  • Cost control: the biggest lever was the cached prompt prefix. A RAG change that looked harmless broke the cache and tripled the per-minute cost; measured on the bill, rolled back the same day. Model choices per step (callback vs. live call) are tracked with a rollback ready.
  • Reporting: period reports for the customer-facing team with request topics, events and per-call descriptions, built from real call data; metrics computed without the "unrated" calls so a quiet week doesn't look like a quality drop.

Result

  • Three weeks of August 2026: 2,478 calls, about 95 a day on the safety line, clean language handling confirmed on live calls.
  • Every production workflow alerts on failure; the team learns about an outage from Telegram, not from customers.
