HighLevel (GoHighLevel) Review for Agencies in 2026: Features, Pricing and Fit

The short version. HighLevel (also called GoHighLevel, GHL) is an all-in-one CRM and marketing platform built around agencies that manage many client accounts. Plans on its pricing page, checked on 12 November 2026: Starter $97, Unlimited $297 and a top tier at $497 per month, plus usage charges for SMS, calls, email and AI. It fits agencies and service businesses that want one login for CRM, funnels, calendars, messaging and automations. It is a poor fit for a team that only needs a simple sales pipeline.

What is HighLevel, and who is it built for?

HighLevel started as a tool for marketing agencies and still carries that shape. The unit of work is the sub-account (HighLevel's docs call it a "location"): a separate workspace for one client, with its own contacts, pipelines, calendars, workflows and users. The agency sits one level above and controls billing, branding, templates and which features each client sees.

That structure is the main thing to understand. Most CRMs are designed for one company that talks to its own customers. HighLevel is designed for a company that runs other companies' customer communication. If you only manage your own pipeline, you will pay for a lot of agency machinery you never touch.

What can HighLevel do?

The platform bundles tools that agencies usually stitch together from four or five products:

  • CRM and pipelines. Contacts, companies, opportunities on kanban-style pipelines, tasks, custom fields and custom objects (up to 10 per sub-account, and they do not work with bulk messaging or payments).
  • Conversations. One inbox for SMS, email, Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, web chat and calls.
  • Workflows. A visual builder with triggers (form submitted, appointment booked, tag added, payment received) and actions (send message, update the pipeline, call a webhook, run custom code).
  • Funnels, websites, forms, calendars. Landing pages and booking pages that write straight into the CRM.
  • AI agents. Conversation AI for chat channels and Voice AI for calls, billed per use or through a per-sub-account AI Employee plan.
  • Payments and invoices, memberships, reputation tools, reporting.

What costs extra?

The monthly fee is only the entry ticket. Phone numbers, SMS, email sending, calls and premium AI are charged by usage from a wallet. Add-ons listed on the same page include:

  • AI Employee: $50 (Growth) or $97 (Unlimited) per sub-account per month.
  • White-label mobile app: $497 per month.
  • Branded client portal app: $49 per sub-account per month.
  • HIPAA compliance: $297 per month. Premium support: $500 per month.

What are white-label and SaaS mode?

White-label means your clients see your brand, not HighLevel's: your domain, logo and login page. The desktop app version is part of the $297 plan; a branded mobile app is a separate $497 monthly add-on.

SaaS mode turns the platform into a product you resell. A client pays you through your own Stripe account, HighLevel creates a sub-account from a template (a "snapshot"), and the features you chose for that plan switch on. You can set plans with their own prices, trial periods and feature limits. According to HighLevel's help center, usage rebilling with your own markup is part of this tier, while the $297 plan rebills at cost only. Some help articles disagree about exactly which tier unlocks which SaaS feature, so confirm in a trial account before you promise a client or a business model anything.

What are the pros and cons?

Where does HighLevel work well?

  • One contact record across everything. Fewer integrations to maintain than a stack of separate tools.
  • Snapshots. You build a client setup once (pipelines, workflows, forms, templates) and copy it into every new account.
  • Flat price for unlimited clients on the higher plans, which is hard to match with per-seat CRMs.
  • An open API. API v2 allows 100 requests per 10 seconds and 200,000 per day per app and location, which is enough for most custom integrations.

Where does it fall short?

  • Usage costs sneak up. SMS, calls and AI are billed separately, so a busy sub-account can cost more than the subscription.
  • US SMS needs registration. A2P registration is required for compliant texting in the US, and approval takes effort.
  • Channel gaps. Telegram and Viber are not built in. If your clients live there, you need a custom connection.
  • Country coverage varies. Telephony and number availability depend on the country, so check your target markets first.
  • Reporting quirks. Revenue on dashboards counts won opportunities, not payments. Use the payments widgets for actual money.
  • Simple e-signatures. Documents and contracts cover basic signing, not qualified signatures required by some jurisdictions.
  • Moving target. Features ship weekly. Help articles lag behind and occasionally contradict each other.

Frequently asked questions

Is HighLevel the same as GoHighLevel?

Yes. The company rebranded to HighLevel, but most agencies and search results still say GoHighLevel or GHL.

Does HighLevel have a free plan?

No. It has a 14-day free trial on the listed plans. Billing starts when the trial ends unless you cancel.

Can I use HighLevel for only one business?

Yes. Starter is positioned for one business or a few clients, but you still pay agency-style pricing for it.

Do I need SaaS mode to white-label?

No. A white-label desktop app is listed on the $297 plan. SaaS mode, which automates selling and provisioning accounts, belongs to the $497 tier according to the pricing page.

Can HighLevel connect to other tools?

Yes, through native integrations, the app marketplace, webhooks, custom code actions and the public API. [internal link: Zapier workflow examples]

Bottom line

HighLevel makes sense when you run client accounts at scale and want one platform you can brand and resell. Count usage fees in the budget from day one, test your own process during the trial, and treat feature lists in any review, including this one, as a snapshot of the day it was written.
